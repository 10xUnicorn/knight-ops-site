About the Founder

Daniel Knight

Founder & Systems Architect at Knight Ops

I don't come from a tech background. I come from operations. I've run teams, managed P&Ls, and built the dashboards and systems that turned struggling businesses into top performers. That operational DNA is what makes Knight Ops different from every other dev shop.

I started at Hertz Car Sales as a salesperson, moved to finance manager in six months, then store manager, then regional manager. As store manager, I built the scorecards and accountability systems that made my location the #1 store in the country across every KPI. As regional manager, I scaled that playbook company-wide.

After that, I became VP of Operations for a fractional CFO overseeing 7 companies. I managed operations across all of them, including a wealth management firm managing ~$100M in assets. When one advisor sold his book of business, I moved with it to the acquiring company and retained 95% of clients through the transition. Then I streamlined operations with systems and dashboards that produced $4.5M in additional revenue that same year, and took nightly client review prep from 4 hours down to 30 minutes before offloading it entirely.

I founded Knight Ops because I kept seeing the same problem: real organizations with teams, revenue, and real operational complexity crushed by disconnected tools and generic solutions built by developers who never ran a business. I build differently because I think differently. Operations first, then technology.

Why I Work Directly With Every Client

When you work with Knight Ops, you work with me. Not a junior developer, not a project manager playing telephone. The person who architects your system is the person who builds it. That's how you get something that actually fits your business instead of a generic template with your logo on it.

What I Believe

The right system at the right time changes everything. It's not about technology — it's about removing the friction between where you are and where you're headed. Every system I build is designed to create leverage: more output with less input, more clarity with less noise, more growth with less chaos.

Common Questions

Straight Answers

Who is Daniel Knight?

Daniel Knight is the founder of Knight Ops and a Fractional Chief AI Operations Officer. He came from operations, not engineering: salesperson to regional manager at Hertz Car Sales, then VP of Operations for a fractional CFO overseeing seven companies, including a wealth management firm managing roughly $100M in assets. He has deployed 50+ business systems representing $200M+ in business impact.

What does Knight Ops do?

Knight Ops designs, deploys and evolves intelligent business operating systems for organizations, and provides embedded Fractional Chief AI Operations Officer leadership to own them. It is not a development agency and not an AI consultancy. The system is deployed progressively by layer, so the organization gets working value throughout the build rather than one handoff at the end.

What is a Fractional Chief AI Operations Officer?

A Fractional Chief AI Operations Officer is an embedded senior leader who owns an organization's AI and operations strategy, directs the build of its systems, and leads the team through adoption, without the cost of a full-time executive hire. The distinction from a consultant is ownership: an advisor recommends, an owner is accountable for the outcome. Knight Ops engagements start at $7,500 per month.

Who does Knight Ops work with?

Organizations with employees, contractors and delivery teams whose operations have outgrown the tools they were built on. Common signals: founder dependency, manual processes that don't survive growth, disconnected tools with duplicated data, delivery that depends on someone remembering, and leadership that cannot see margin, utilization and pipeline in one place. Not solo operators or side businesses.

Does the client own the system Knight Ops builds?

Yes, completely. Repository, database, hosting and domain are transferred to the client, along with a written continuity brief naming a successor developer. Clients own 100% of the code. There is no platform to stay subscribed to in order to keep what was built.

How does working with Knight Ops start?

Three steps, and there is no fourth. A Tech Discovery Call establishes fit in 20 to 30 minutes. A Systems Blueprint Session, by invitation, maps what is actually broken, what should be built and in what order, and the organization keeps that architecture whether or not Knight Ops builds it. Then the system itself is deployed.

What makes Knight Ops different from a development agency?

Operations first, then technology. Most development shops are staffed by engineers who have never run a P&L, so they build what was asked for rather than what the business needs. Daniel ran the operations before he built the software, and works directly on every engagement rather than routing it through a junior developer.
